Firefighter Rescue Survey

  • 540 structure-fire incidents with rescues.
  • 1,033 civilians rescued—an average of 11.5 per day.

  • Multifamily dwellings averaged 3.2 victims per incident.

First 100 refined stats

  • Emergency: floor fall 31.5%; lost/disoriented 23.6%; collapse 18.0%; cut off or overrun 10.1%.
  • Structure: homes 73.2%; low-rise apartments 9.8%; strip malls 7.3%; box stores 9.8%.
  • Emergency button: 86.4% did not activate it.
  • IC training: 57.7% lacked RIT/mayday-management training.
  • Who affected rescue: self-rescue 35.4%; own crew 28%; another interior company 17.1%; exterior company 11%; RIT 8.5%.
  • Egress: 65.8% were within 20 feet of the removal egress.

What this means for usRehearse early mayday transmission, emergency-button use, decisive defensive transitions, and removal paths that exploit the distressed firefighter’s own crew and nearby companies.
12,851 departments · 2015–2021

Project Mayday

  • 30% of maydays occurred from 1–3 a.m.; 57% involved the first unit.
  • 52% occurred 20–35 minutes after arrival.
  • Survival path: self-rescue 36%, own crew 26%, another interior crew 25%.
  • RIT accounted for 7% of rescues.
  • One in nine RITs deployed for rescue had its own mayday.

Project Mayday analysis ↗

What this means for usBuild mayday prevention into first-unit discipline: complete the 360, track crews beyond the 20-minute mark, and practice crew-based rescue—not RIT reliance alone.

Scope and method

This is a curated operational review for January 1–October 8, 2026, built from the linked reports. Dashboard rescues are structure-fire incidents only; drowning responses are excluded. Close calls are notable near-misses reported by FirefighterCloseCalls.com, not a complete national census. Florida incidents are prioritized. Mayday scanner items are explicitly marked unverified; USFA statuses remain provisional where applicable.

Prepared for company training and after-action discussion.Verify local SOGs and current NFPA editions before drilling.
